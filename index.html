Maison Laminée

Layer by layer

Three letter folds, chilled between each turn: twenty-seven sheets of Normandy butter, none of them touching.

Shaped by hand

Cut into long triangles and rolled from base to tip, so the layers spiral around the centre.

Baked to rise

Eighteen minutes at 190 °C. The water in the butter turns to steam and lifts every layer apart.

Made to flake

Flour, water, milk, sugar, salt, yeast and butter at 82 % fat. Nothing else, and nothing to hide.

Layers worth breaking

Warm from the oven, Tuesday to Sunday from seven.

See today’s counter

Three days, one croissant

  1. On the first evening, flour, milk, sugar, salt and yeast are mixed into a soft dough and left to rest overnight at 4 °C, so it is cold and calm by morning.

  2. The next morning, a sheet of butter is locked inside and folded three times, an hour in the cold between each turn, then cut, rolled and left to proof.

  3. At seven on the third day, the first tray comes out of the oven and goes straight to the counter, still crackling as it cools.
